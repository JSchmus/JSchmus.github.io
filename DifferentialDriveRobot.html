Webcam-Localized Differential Drive Rover

Fall 2025 · Biola University
MATLAB Simulink and Stateflow Computer Vision Localization

Overview

We developed a differential-drive rover capable of navigating to a target, retrieving an object, and delivering it to a designated location. We derived and validated the rover's forward and inverse kinematics in MATLAB/Simulink before deploying them to hardware.

Approach

Hand-tuned PI controllers used wheel encoder measurements as feedback to regulate heading and travel distance, while a Stateflow state machine coordinated the navigation sequence. An overhead webcam provided the rover's initial position and heading by identifying three colored markers on the chassis: marker centroid gave position, marker geometry gave heading. After calibration, this state estimate was passed to the control program before autonomous movement. The rover then relied on wheel encoder feedback rather than continuous visual feedback. The vision system required calibrating arena dimensions, reference points, and color thresholds.

Results

The rover didn't achieve the desired heading accuracy, primarily due to differences in motor variation and quality as well as wheel slip. We replaced one motor and repeated characterization, but the errors persisted. Our proposed next step was adding an IMU for direct orientation measurement, reducing reliance on wheel-based encoder counts that couldn't fully account for slip and motor behavior.
