SPARROE: Low-Cost Quadcopter

Summer 2024 · Biola University
Component Selection and Assembly Electrical Architecture

Overview

Five of us built SPARROE as a $56 alternative to the $180 drones on the market. Over six weeks during my freshman year, we completed a team build spanning chassis, electronics, and software. I selected the motor driver boards, battery, and boost module, planned the electrical architecture, and soldered and assembled the flight electronics onto the frame.

Low-Cost Drone Poster

Design Reflection

If I were approaching this same project as a senior, I'd perform a more rigorous power and thrust analysis before selecting components. Many of the components we sourced from Amazon lacked complete datasheets, making it difficult to determine important specifications such as motor stall current, driver operating limits and voltage drop, and boost converter capabilities. I also initially underestimated the importance of battery discharge ratings and current requirements. We experienced inconsistent motor speeds during testing, highlighting the need to account for battery performance under load and voltage losses through the L298N motor drivers. I'd prioritize components with reliable documentation and select the motors, drivers, and battery as a complete system using measured current and thrust requirements, with sufficient margin for peak loads.
