Drone Swarm Capstone

2026–2027 · Biola University · Capstone IN PROGRESS
Skybrush ArduPilot RTK GNSS

Overview

Our four-person team is building a new generation of Biola-owned swarm drones: small quadcopters with onboard LEDs that fly together to put on light shows on campus and promote Biola's Robotics Program. We are starting with a 2–3 drone prototype, with a long-term goal of 50–100 drones.

My Role

I work on the show software and communications side: choosing the drone show software, designing how the ground station talks to the drones, and researching the networking and positioning hardware.

Key Requirements

  • Show software compatible with our flight controller (Pixhawk 6C Mini with ArduPilot) that can scale to 50–100 drones
  • A private ground-to-drone network that keeps working safely if the Wi-Fi link drops
  • Shows that stay under the FAA 400 ft limit and are legible to the audience

Approach

System Architecture

The team selected Skybrush for drone-show planning and monitoring, with Skybrush Studio used to prepare shows and Skybrush Live used for ground-station operations. The selected flight-control hardware is the Pixhawk 6C Mini running ArduPilot. We are evaluating a private Wi-Fi network for show uploads and ground-station communication, along with a secondary radio and GNSS/RTK positioning hardware. A key design question is how these links will divide responsibilities and how the system will behave if either link becomes unavailable. The final architecture will depend on hardware compatibility and testing.

Key Decisions

I compared Skybrush with DroneShowSoftware, considering cost and project requirements. Skybrush was the better fit for our initial small fleet and budget. We also selected the Pixhawk 6C Mini with ArduPilot after checking flight-controller compatibility with the show software. These decisions narrowed the options for the remaining communication and positioning hardware.

Progress So Far

So far, our team has compared drone-show software, selected the flight controller and flight software, and developed a preliminary communication architecture. I am currently researching suitable Wi-Fi, secondary-radio, and GNSS/RTK hardware. One important constraint is that a secondary radio link may not provide the same two-way communication as Wi-Fi, so telemetry and loss-of-link behavior need to be considered separately. The architecture still needs to be validated with the actual hardware and software configuration.

Next Steps

  • Research something about what happens if wifi is lost
  • Finalize router, radio, and GNSS receiver recommendations
  • Test the communication link on the prototype drones before the December prototype demonstration
